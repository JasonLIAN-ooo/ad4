Lian, J. (2026). “Looking North” and “Resisting North”: Guangdong–Hong Kong imaginaries in In-Laws, Out-Laws, the first southern New Year film in mainland China. Manuscript under review.

Supervisor: Hui HU, Associate Professor, South China Agricultural University.

  • Questions： Why did it fare poorly at the Hong Kong box office yet perform relatively well in Guangdong? Why did the “southern” affinity fail to guarantee the success of the Guangdong–Hong Kong film co-production?
  • Method： Drew on a cross-cultural production perspective, reconstructed the entire process through close reading and comparison of the television and film texts combined with related interviews and reports, and analyzed it in the contexts of Lunar New Year film development in Hong Kong and mainland China, Raymond Wong’s personal creative trajectory, and other related contexts.
  • Key findings： For Hong Kong, not only the “North” but also the “South” is the Other who is being looked up to and needed to resist. As far as Guangdong is concerned, it has completed a psychological imagination of “identity reversal” in the film, but such imagination is still not separated from Hong Kong as a reference system, reflecting complex identity anxiety.
  • Innovation： Refined the concept of the mainland to the south of the mainland and the north of the mainland, and provided another analytical path for understanding Hong Kong-ness in the co-productions. Filled the research gap of the southern New Year film in mainland China.

Lian, J. (2026). Motivation, practices and space of “wandering” in Zhang Lü’s and Hong Sang-soo’s films. Manuscript under review.

  • Questions： How do the differences in the wanderer’s motivations, practices, and spaces between the Chinese Korean director and the South Korean director reflect aspects of the experience of modernity in East Asian cinema?
  • Method： Taking Dooman River, Night and Day, and other representative films as texts for comparison, this study comprehensively employs interdisciplinary perspectives from film studies, cultural studies, and other fields to conduct an analysis.
  • Key findings： Zhang Lü’s films point to a tracing of the past and present a profound sense of history. Hong Sang-soo’s arise from present desires and manifest an isomorphism between daily space and ethical boundaries. These constitute two forms of the experience of “homelessness” in East Asian cinema: one is “historical homelessness,” and the other is “everyday homelessness.”
  • Innovation： This study enriched the comparative map of experiences of cinematic modernity in East Asia.

Regular Project of the Guangdong Provincial Philosophy and Social Sciences Planning Programme
Supervisor: Hui HU, Associate Professor, South China Agricultural University.

  • Synopsis

Cultural Heritage & Other Tracks

A Digital Interactive Theatre of Cultural Artefacts Set in the Singaporean Kopitiam

Sep. 2026 – Present

Interdisciplinary Self-Organizing Cultural Relics Research Project.

  • Synopsis
    This project uses the digital interactive theatre to make cultural relics perceptible rather than constructed, to show its autonomy. The scene is set in the Singaporean Kopitiam, a coffee shop, to discuss the relationships between migration, labor, and public space. It is currently in the narrative-building stage.
  • Main Contributions
    • Reviewed the literature and organized existing museum collection records and other materials, placed coffee shops back into colonial trade and other structures for examination, avoided obfuscation by nostalgic narratives, and made the topic no longer too broad.
    • Started from questions such as “which objects might appear in the same scene,” refined the selection criteria, proposed a method of linking stories through core objects, solved the problem of fragmented stories caused by too many objects, and advanced the project to the verification stage of “which objects are related to one another.”
